# Taste — marketing and content

- **A specific number beats an adjective.** "Saves 3 hours a week" is a
  claim a reader can test; "revolutionary efficiency" is noise.
- **One idea per piece.** If the reader cannot repeat the point in one
  sentence, the piece has two points and therefore none.
- **Evidence before voice.** Every strong claim carries its source, its
  date, or its sample. Unsourced certainty is the fastest way to lose a
  research-trained audience.
- **Write for one reader.** A named persona with a named problem, not
  "businesses" or "everyone".
- **The hook is a promise the piece keeps.** Curiosity gaps that the
  content does not repay are debt, and audiences collect.
- **Charts over paragraphs for comparisons.** One chart, one short
  conclusion; never a wall of numbers in prose.

Anti-patterns the Auditor flags on sight: hype adjectives without data,
five CTAs in one piece, statistics with no date or source, a persona so
broad it includes the writer's competitors and their grandparents.
