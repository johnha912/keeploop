# Taste - marketing research (consumer insight report)

- **Start from the decision.** Each module opens with the business question
  and the decision it informs. A chart nobody will act on is cost, not insight.
- **Answer first.** Lead with the one-sentence answer, then the evidence,
  then method and limits (the pyramid principle).
- **Action titles.** A chart or section title states the takeaway as a
  sentence ("Shein leads on price but trails on quality"), never a topic
  label ("Brand perception").
- **Neutral listening.** Search on brand names, not on loaded terms
  ("Shein scam") that pre-select the answer. Measure every brand with the
  same dictionary, window and sources.
- **Quotes by rule.** Choose verbatims by a stated rule (for example, closest
  to the cell's median sentiment), never the most dramatic one.
- **Name the proxy.** Social data counts people who post, not buyers.
  Mention share is a proxy for awareness, "I bought" posts a proxy for
  penetration; say so where the number appears.
- **Base and interval on every share.** Show n and a 95% interval; do not
  report a share on fewer than 30 cases. A gap inside overlapping intervals
  is not a finding.
- **Funnel order.** Awareness, penetration, profile, positioning,
  perception, segmentation: each step narrows the question the next answers.
- **So-what and now-what.** Every module ends with what it means for the
  client and what to do about it.
- **Grey plus one colour.** Charts are mostly grey with one accent on the
  point; label data directly; no chart junk (Knaflic, Storytelling with Data).

Anti-patterns the Auditor flags on sight: a number that is not in the
analysis output, a share without its base, a proxy presented as the real
measure, the most extreme quote passed off as typical, a headline the
interval does not support, objectives rewritten to fit what the data
happened to show (brief.md is protected for that reason).
