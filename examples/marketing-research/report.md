# Fieldnote Apparel: What Gen Z and Millennial fast-fashion shoppers in the US and Canada say about the brands (fall 2026)

## Summary: Fieldnote should enter next year with the Zara-led talk segment, positioned on trend and fit, and validate that choice with a survey first

Public Reddit talk is a signal, not a measurement of shoppers. Within that limit, the Zara-led group of authors is the segment where Fieldnote's mid-priced, trend-led offer meets the most open dissatisfaction: Zara draws a higher share of negative sentences than Aritzia or Uniqlo, and Zara is discussed mostly for price and quality. Segments are weakly separated, so this is a hypothesis for the next study, not a verdict.

Target segment: Segment 1 (Zara-led, fit/sizing-minded; 83 authors, 15.8% of eligible authors, n=525, 95% CI 12.9% to 19.2%)

## 1. Top-of-mind: Uniqlo is named first in recommendation threads far more often than the other four core brands, but the sample leans to menswear

Question: Which brands do people name first when asked where to shop?

Decision: Which competitors Fieldnote must displace in the shopper's first thought, and which it can treat as secondary.

- Base: replies that name at least one brand, n=217, taken from 2140 top-level replies in 60 recommendation threads. 48 of the 60 threads are in a menswear subreddit and 12 in femalefashionadvice. All shares below are a proxy for salience, not measured awareness.
- Uniqlo: first-named in 42.4% of brand-naming replies (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 36.0% to 49.0%); named at all in 43.8% (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 37.3% to 50.4%).
- Zara: first-named in 3.2% (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 1.6% to 6.5%); named at all in 3.7% (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 1.9% to 7.1%).
- H&M: first-named in 1.8% (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 0.7% to 4.6%); named at all in 2.3% (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 1.0% to 5.3%).
- Aritzia: first-named in 0.9% (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 0.3% to 3.3%); named at all in 1.8% (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 0.7% to 4.6%).
- Shein: first-named in 0.9% (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 0.3% to 3.3%); named at all in 1.4% (proxy; n=217, 95% CI 0.5% to 4.0%).

So what: In this sample only Uniqlo stands apart from the other four, whose intervals overlap. Because most threads are menswear, the result reflects menswear-friendly brands and says little about Gen Z or Millennial women.

Recommendation: Fieldnote should not read this module as a ranking of the womenswear market, and should add an aided and unaided awareness question to the validating survey before setting any awareness target.

## 2. Penetration: Self-reported purchase talk is similar across the five brands, with overlapping intervals

Question: Who says they bought or own each brand?

Decision: Whether Fieldnote is entering a market where one brand already holds most ownership talk.

- Base: authors who mentioned any brand, n=1453. Each share is a penetration proxy: authors using an ownership or purchase phrase, not confirmed buyers.
- Aritzia: 1.7% (proxy; n=1453, 95% CI 1.2% to 2.5%).
- Zara: 2.3% (proxy; n=1453, 95% CI 1.6% to 3.2%).
- H&M: 2.3% (proxy; n=1453, 95% CI 1.7% to 3.3%).
- Shein: 2.0% (proxy; n=1453, 95% CI 1.4% to 2.9%).
- Uniqlo: 2.3% (proxy; n=1453, 95% CI 1.6% to 3.2%).

So what: The values are small by construction and the five intervals overlap, so this data does not separate the brands on ownership.

Recommendation: Fieldnote should not claim a penetration gap against any competitor from this data and should measure claimed ownership in the survey.

## 3. Profiling: Few authors report an age, and among those who do, the Gen Z and Millennial shares overlap, so the audience cannot be described reliably

Question: Who is talking, by age, gender and country?

Decision: Whether the listening data can stand in for Fieldnote's target shopper in the US and Canada.

- Coverage of self-reported age is low: 33 of 1453 brand-mentioning authors, 2.3% (n=1453, 95% CI 1.6% to 3.2%). Among all authors it is 0.6% (n=16697, 95% CI 0.5% to 0.7%).
- Among the 33 authors who gave an age: Gen Z 57.6% (n=33, 95% CI 40.8% to 72.8%) and Millennial 39.4% (n=33, 95% CI 24.7% to 56.3%). The median stated age is 25.0.
- Gender: base too small (13 authors reported one). Country: base too small (21 authors reported one; Canada and US cannot be compared). No percentage is given for either.

So what: The generation split rests on a few dozen people, and the intervals for Gen Z and Millennial overlap. The data cannot separate the US from Canada.

Recommendation: Fieldnote should draw its US and Canada audience profile from the survey, quota-sampled on age, gender and country, not from these posts.

## 4. Positioning: Shein is discussed more for sustainability and ethics, and Uniqlo more for style and trend, than the other brands

Question: Which attributes is each brand talked about for?

Decision: Which attribute territory Fieldnote can claim that is not already tied to a competitor.

Positioning shares carry no interval in the analysis, so the evidence is given as counts of single-brand sentences that mention each attribute (a sentence can hit several). Counts for the five core brands, in the order price/value, quality/durability, fit/sizing, style/trend, sustainability/ethics:

- Aritzia: 28, 20, 20, 21, 3.
- Zara: 70, 54, 34, 51, 17.
- H&M: 38, 29, 22, 31, 10.
- Shein: 54, 62, 20, 41, 45.
- Uniqlo: 67, 57, 44, 76, 10.
- The brand-by-attribute pattern is not independent overall (chi-square 100.75, df 42, n=1100; indicative only, because one author can contribute several hits). After a Bonferroni correction, three core-brand cells depart from expectation: Shein on sustainability/ethics (45 observed vs 21.6 expected, z=6.03), Shein on fit/sizing (20 vs 36.4, z=-3.36) and Uniqlo on style/trend (76 vs 56.5, z=3.37). Aritzia, Zara and H&M have no cell that survives the correction.
- Aritzia has the smallest counts of the five, so its profile is the least certain.

One quote per brand, all from the price/value cell:

> "If you want to splurge (200$ range per piece), try Aritzia!" (Aritzia, price/value)

> "I still wear Zara and Banana Republic stuff from 20 years sgo because I don't treat any of my clothes like they're cheap." (Zara, price/value)

> "I have one acrylic sweater that I sort of like, but it was a $15 H&M buy." (H&M, price/value)

> "I do think SHEIN overall has much lower quality far more consistently, but I agree that mainstream retail quality has declined aggressively while prices climb." (Shein, price/value)

> "Uniqlo mini tee is my favorite at an affordable price point" (Uniqlo, price/value)

Quote rule: each cell's quotes are the sentences closest to the cell's median sentiment, one per author; from the price/value cell I took one per brand, picking the one that reads as a complete sentence without text-encoding damage. This is an editorial choice among rule-selected quotes.

So what: Price/value and quality/durability come up for every core brand, so they do not differentiate. Style/trend is already associated with Uniqlo, and Shein carries the ethics conversation. Fit/sizing is a lower share of Shein talk than expected, which leaves it open as a territory.

Recommendation: Fieldnote should test a trend-led, fit-reliable proposition against Zara and Uniqlo, and avoid competing on price talk, where the five brands already sit.

## 5. Perception: Shein and Zara draw more negative sentences than Aritzia, Uniqlo or H&M, but the tool understates negativity

Question: How positive or negative is the talk about each brand?

Decision: Which competitor weaknesses Fieldnote can credibly exploit, and which brand strengths it can borrow.

- Base: single-brand sentences scored with VADER. Net sentiment is positive minus negative as a share of all sentences.
- Aritzia: positive 48.0% (n=221, 95% CI 41.5% to 54.5%), negative 6.8% (n=221, 95% CI 4.2% to 10.9%); net sentiment 0.412.
- Zara: positive 44.9% (n=392, 95% CI 40.0% to 49.8%), negative 18.4% (n=392, 95% CI 14.8% to 22.5%); net sentiment 0.265.
- H&M: positive 43.7% (n=252, 95% CI 37.7% to 49.8%), negative 10.7% (n=252, 95% CI 7.5% to 15.1%); net sentiment 0.329.
- Shein: positive 43.8% (n=356, 95% CI 38.8% to 49.0%), negative 23.6% (n=356, 95% CI 19.5% to 28.3%); net sentiment 0.202.
- Uniqlo: positive 51.0% (n=504, 95% CI 46.6% to 55.3%), negative 7.5% (n=504, 95% CI 5.5% to 10.2%); net sentiment 0.435.
- Positive shares overlap across the five brands. Negative shares do not overlap between Shein or Zara and Aritzia or Uniqlo.
- Check on the tool: VADER agreed with hand labels on 64% of 50 sentences (n=50, 95% CI 50.1% to 75.9%; kappa 0.45, moderate; one labeller). Of 14 sentences a human judged negative, VADER labelled 4 negative. Net sentiment therefore likely understates negativity, and attribute-level sentiment cells are mostly too small to read.
- App Store review text could not be collected. Store-level US averages on a five-point scale are 4.91 (Aritzia), 4.82 (Zara), 4.84 (H&M), 4.73 (Shein) and 4.78 (Uniqlo). These are all-time store figures, not a sample, and reflect the app and delivery as much as the clothes.

So what: The gap in negative talk is the most defensible perception finding here, but the tool misses much criticism and the sentiment is toward sentences, not buyers.

Recommendation: Fieldnote should treat Zara's higher share of negative talk as a lead to investigate, not a proven weakness, and should measure brand attribute ratings in the survey.

## 6. Segmentation: Five talk groups emerge, but they overlap, and the Zara-led group is the suggested first target

Question: Which groups of people have similar brand and attribute talk, and which should Fieldnote target first?

Decision: Which segment Fieldnote builds its first-year US and Canada plan around.

- Base: 525 authors with at least two brand-mention sentences, grouped into five segments. Cluster separation is weak (silhouette 0.189 at five groups, the highest of the options tried), so the groups are talk patterns, not validated shopper types.
- Segment 1, Zara-led, fit/sizing-minded: 83 authors, 15.8% (n=525, 95% CI 12.9% to 19.2%); net sentiment 0.292; negative 17.5% (n=212, 95% CI 12.9% to 23.1%).
- Segment 2, Aritzia-led, shipping/returns-minded: 123 authors, 23.4% (n=525, 95% CI 20.0% to 27.2%); net sentiment 0.428; negative 6.7% (n=299, 95% CI 4.4% to 10.1%).
- Segment 3, Shein-led, sustainability/ethics-minded: 113 authors, 21.5% (n=525, 95% CI 18.2% to 25.2%); net sentiment 0.197; negative 18.4% (n=239, 95% CI 14.0% to 23.8%).
- Segment 4, Uniqlo-led, style/trend-minded: 103 authors, 19.6% (n=525, 95% CI 16.4% to 23.2%); net sentiment 0.405; negative 12.4% (n=210, 95% CI 8.6% to 17.5%).
- Segment 5, H&M-led, quality/durability-minded: 103 authors, 19.6% (n=525, 95% CI 16.4% to 23.2%); net sentiment 0.259; negative 15.1% (n=212, 95% CI 10.9% to 20.5%).
- Generation is known for only a handful of authors per segment, so no generation profile of any segment can be given (base too small).

So what: Segment 1 is the smallest of the five but is built around the brand closest to Fieldnote's mid-priced, trend-led positioning, and its talk carries a higher share of negative sentences than the Aritzia-led or Uniqlo-led groups. Segment 2 is larger but already positive about its brand, leaving less to win. These intervals for segment size overlap, so size alone does not decide the choice.

Recommendation: Fieldnote should target the Zara-led segment first as a working hypothesis, with fit and sizing as the lead proposition, and confirm the segment's size, age and country mix in a survey before committing the plan.

## Method and limits

Evidence is social listening, not a survey: public Reddit posts and comments from November 2025 to October 2026 through the Arctic Shift archive, App Store store-level ratings, and Wikipedia page views. The analysis covers 29507 Reddit items from 16697 authors; 1865 items and 1453 authors mention a brand, giving 2779 brand-mention sentences, of which 2100 name a single brand and 679 name several and are excluded from sentiment and attributes. Cross-brand modules use general subreddits only, so fan communities do not favour their own brand. Brands were found by name search with one dictionary applied to all five core brands.

Limits to keep in mind:

- Social media is not a representative sample of North American Gen Z and Millennial shoppers; Reddit is self-selected, and who posts there is not measured in this data.
- The collection was time-boxed and partial. The general-subreddit sample is mainly femalefashionadvice keyword search plus recommendation-thread replies, and 10 time windows were given up after retries.
- 48 of the 60 recommendation threads are menswear, so top-of-mind results lean to menswear-friendly brands.
- Top-of-mind, penetration and positioning are proxies, not survey measures.
- Age, gender and country are self-reported by few authors, so generation and country splits are mostly base too small, and Canada cannot be separated from the US.
- VADER agreement with hand labels is moderate (64%, n=50, 95% CI 50.1% to 75.9%) and it understates negativity.
- Segments are weak (low silhouette) and describe talk patterns, not shopper types.
- App Store review text failed to collect; only store-level ratings exist, and no review sentiment is reported.
- No subreddit with data exists for Zara or H&M, so their within-brand context is missing.

Next step: a fielded survey on a representative sample of Gen Z and Millennial shoppers in the US and Canada, to validate awareness, ownership, profile, attribute ratings and the size of the Zara-led segment. Pricing tests, ad creative and product concepts remain out of scope.
